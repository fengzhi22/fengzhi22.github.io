Prior research documents that linguistic tone is incrementally informative about stock returns, but does not explain the mechanism by which investors can assess its credibility. This study proposes management earnings forecast as one verification mechanism to the credibility of tone and finds that different constructs of management earnings forecast moderate the price effect of tone in different directions and to different degrees. Furthermore, this study documents that characteristics of the linguistic tone itself also affect investors’ pricing of tone. Specifically, they find that the sign agreement between management forecast surprise and tone increase the pricing effect, and this increase is more pronounced when the sign agreement is positive. While the imprecision of management forecast attenuates the pricing effect. Finally, the use of hyperbole (exaggeration in tone) will also attenuates the pricing effect.

<h5>Theory</h5>
Narratives information are costless to convey and hard to verify ex-post, which is why it may reduce to cheap talk. Management earnings forecast can serve as a verification tool of tone because it is an ex-post verifiable signal, which is of higher quality. Prior literature establishes that in the presence of two signals, the higher quality signal can add credence to the potentially lower quality signal. In an unbundled management forecast, these two signals (narrative tone and quantitative earnings forecast) are both present, but the latter is subsequently verified by earnings realization, which adds credibility to the former.

This study is also interesting in documenting the interaction between narrative and numerical information:

<i>"Prior management forecast research documents the effects of supplemental disclosures on the pricing of quantitative forecast news. We complement this finding by documenting the reverse effect of quantitative management forecast news on the pricing of supplemental linguistic tone.”</i>

<h5>Writing</h5>
This paper is very well-written, as it contains many detailed explanations in data selection process (large firm sample bias) and the motivations behind model specifications (why cluster). It also inspires me about how to make use of LM certainty and litigation construct.
